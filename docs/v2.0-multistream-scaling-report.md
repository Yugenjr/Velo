# Velo V2.0 Multi-Stream Scaling Report

## 1. Test Environment
- **OS**: Windows 11 (10.0.26200)
- **CPU**: Intel64 Family 6 Model 186 Stepping 2
- **GPU**: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 A Laptop GPU (1, 4093.5 MB VRAM)
- **Python**: 3.13.9
- **Torch**: 2.6.0+cu124
- **CUDA**: 12.4

## 2. Benchmark Methodology
The benchmark measures the absolute maximum decoding and hardware throughput capacity of the `velo.RtpReceiver` and NVDEC hardware pipeline. 
A synthetic standard Annex-B H.264 stream is generated using PyAV (`preset=ultrafast`, `tune=zerolatency`).
Multiple `RealH264StreamSource` workers are spawned in parallel background threads to push unthrottled H.264 NAL units into isolated `RtpReceiver` instances.
The main benchmark monitors the aggregate frames decoded within a strict 3-second window. By pushing packets as fast as possible without artificial delay, we measure the absolute ceiling of the NVDEC engine across varying degrees of concurrency.

### Benchmark Correctness Fix
An initial correctness audit revealed that PyAV's ultrafast profile generated multiple VCL NAL units per frame. A legacy artificial sleep of 33ms was being applied per-NAL unit rather than per-frame, accidentally rate-limiting the input to ~2.3 FPS and hiding true hardware capacity. The artificial sleep was removed to measure raw unthrottled throughput.

## 3. Raw Measured Results & Scaling Analysis

### 720p (1280x720) Scaling

| Stream Count | Total Decoded | Aggregate FPS | Average Per-Stream FPS | CPU Usage | GPU VRAM | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Stream** | 483 | 160.9 FPS | 160.9 FPS | 22.6% | 0.0 MB* | Sustainable |
| **2 Streams** | 948 | 315.8 FPS | 157.9 FPS | 38.5% | 0.0 MB* | Sustainable |
| **4 Streams** | 1883 | 626.7 FPS | 156.8 FPS | 85.1% | 0.0 MB* | Sustainable |
| **8 Streams** | 3767 | 1251.9 FPS | 156.5 FPS | 134.0% | 0.0 MB* | Sustainable |
| **16 Streams** | 5570 | 1682.0 FPS | 105.1 FPS | 120.5% | 0.0 MB* | Sustainable |

**720p Analysis**: Throughput scales perfectly linearly up to 8 streams (maintaining ~156 FPS per stream). At 16 streams, scaling degrades slightly as the aggregate hits 1,682 FPS. However, the system still comfortably yields over 100 FPS per stream, easily sustaining real-time (30 FPS) requirements.

### 1080p (1920x1080) Scaling

| Stream Count | Total Decoded | Aggregate FPS | Average Per-Stream FPS | CPU Usage | GPU VRAM | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Stream** | 440 | 146.6 FPS | 146.6 FPS | 26.0% | 0.0 MB* | Sustainable |
| **2 Streams** | 878 | 292.4 FPS | 146.2 FPS | 41.6% | 0.0 MB* | Sustainable |
| **4 Streams** | 1765 | 587.7 FPS | 146.9 FPS | 68.6% | 0.0 MB* | Sustainable |
| **8 Streams** | 2796 | 915.4 FPS | 114.4 FPS | 85.2% | 0.0 MB* | Sustainable |
| **16 Streams** | 2905 | 905.2 FPS | 56.6 FPS | 92.3% | 0.0 MB* | Sustainable (Bottlenecked) |

**1080p Analysis**: Throughput scales linearly up to 4 streams (maintaining ~146 FPS per stream). At 8 streams, we see the first sign of degradation (aggregate 915 FPS rather than the theoretical 1,173 FPS). At 16 streams, the hardware is completely saturated, capping out at an aggregate of ~905 FPS. Because 905 FPS / 16 streams = ~56 FPS, it is technically still sustainable for 30 FPS realtime workloads, but it represents the absolute ceiling of the current hardware profile.

*\*Note: GPU VRAM tracks PyTorch-managed memory allocations. NVDEC dedicated video memory is managed outside PyTorch's allocator and is consequently not reflected here.*

## 4. Bottleneck Analysis
The primary bottleneck on the test bench is the **NVDEC hardware decoding engine**. At 1080p, the engine fully saturates at a ceiling of ~905 aggregate FPS. Once this boundary is reached (at 8 to 16 streams), adding more streams merely subdivides the available decoding bandwidth, dropping the per-stream FPS linearly. CPU utilization climbs concurrently but is not the limiting factor. 

## 5. Sustainable Stream Capacity
Based strictly on the measured data for the RTX 3050 Laptop GPU:
- **Verified Safe Sustainable Capacity:** 8 streams at 1080p (Yielding 114 FPS per stream, offering massive headroom over 30 FPS).
- **Absolute Maximum Capacity:** 16 streams at 1080p (Yielding 56 FPS per stream). Beyond 16 streams, latency spikes would likely cause realtime WebRTC frame drops.

## 6. Known Limitations & Unverified Configurations
1. GPU memory reporting for the NVDEC decoder block remains invisible to the `RuntimeMetrics` allocator tracker.
2. End-to-end multi-stream VLM pipeline evaluation was tested synthetically (`MockMultimodalAdapter`); real multi-stream SmolVLM inferences will likely hit the 4GB VRAM ceiling well before the NVDEC engine saturates.
3. LiveKit Cloud scaling is untested due to missing credentials.

## 7. Recommended Next Engineering Step
V2.0 multi-stream runtime scaling has dramatically exceeded initial baseline expectations. The immediate next step should be validating **Real Multi-Stream VLM Inference** on a >8GB VRAM GPU testbench to measure how AI scheduling handles hardware saturation when the NVDEC engine and Tensor Cores are running concurrently.
